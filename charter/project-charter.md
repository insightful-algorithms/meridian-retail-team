## Simulation notice

Meridian Retail Group is fictional. Every dataset in this repository is synthetic and generated by code in `/pipeline`. One person plays all five roles below, switching between them in writing. The purpose is to practise and evidence stakeholder management, requirements discipline, and DataOps delivery under realistic conflict, with every disagreement resolved through a logged decision. No real company, customer, or lender is described. The final README repeats this notice.

## 1. Background

Meridian Retail Group Ltd is a UK online retailer of home and lifestyle goods, headquartered in London, with about £60m in annual online revenue. Two customer programmes sit at the centre of this project.

- **Meridian Pay** is an interest-free pay-in-3 product: one third at checkout, one third at day 30, one third at day 60. Meridian provides the credit itself and carries the credit risk. Finance owns its profit and loss.
- **Meridian Circle** is a free loyalty programme launched in February 2025. Customers join when they choose to, so enrolment is staggered. Members earn points and get early access to sales.

Two questions have gone unanswered because nobody owned the analysis.

1. **Checkout.** A redesigned checkout is built and ready to test. Product expects higher completion. Finance expects that a smoother path to Meridian Pay will attract customers who are more likely to miss repayments, and will not accept a conversion number on its own.
2. **Loyalty.** Marketing reports that Circle members spend about 30% more per month than non-members. Nobody knows how much of that gap the programme causes and how much comes from heavy spenders choosing to join.

## 2. Purpose

Stand up a small data team that answers both questions with evidence that Finance, the Business Analyst, and the Product Owner will each accept, delivered through a pipeline that is version-controlled, tested, and reproducible by anyone who clones the repository.

## 4. Scope

**In scope**

- A seeded synthetic data generator for sessions and checkout events, orders, customers, Meridian Pay agreements and repayments, and Circle membership, covering January 2025 to August 2026.
- A pipeline from raw files to staged tables to analysis marts, with automated data quality tests, in version control, run in CI.
- Workstream 1: experiment design document, experiment mart, statistical analysis, guardrail check, ship / no-ship decision.
- Workstream 2: customer-month spend panel, difference-in-differences event study, propensity score matching, diagnostics, placebo and sensitivity checks, business readout.
- Data dictionary and lineage notes, decision log, standup logs, weekly stakeholder emails, sprint artefacts, reflective log, README.

**Out of scope**

- Building or changing the checkout itself. The redesign is treated as already built.
- Credit decisioning for Meridian Pay (scorecards, affordability rules).
- Streaming or real-time data.
- Dashboards. Charts in the reports are enough. A Power BI report is a stretch item only if every milestone lands early.
- Any real customer data, ever.

**Scope changes.** The Business Analyst logs the request with the problem it solves and the acceptance criteria it would need. The Product Owner decides. The outcome goes in `/decisions` and, if accepted, `/backlog`.

## 7. Approach

### 7.1 Data

The generator is seeded and documented, so any reviewer can regenerate the data exactly. It plants a known conversion effect, a known repayment effect, and a known loyalty effect alongside a known self-selection effect. The true values are recorded in `/pipeline/generator/ground_truth.md` and compared with the estimates only in the final readout. This checks that the methods recover what was planted. It does not replace the diagnostics, which are what a real analysis would have to rely on.

The generator also injects realistic faults: duplicate events, missing values, late-arriving repayment records, and weekly and monthly seasonality. The Data Analyst reviews realism in Sprint 1.

### 7.2 Pipeline (DataOps)

- **Layers.** Raw generated files, then staging (typed, deduplicated, one row per event), then marts: an experiment mart (one row per visitor in the test) and a customer-month spend panel.
- **Tooling.** Python 3.13 in a virtual environment; dbt with DuckDB for transformations; dbt tests plus a Great Expectations suite for data quality; pytest for code; GitHub Actions for CI. Subject to Finance sign-off on cost (D-002, section 9).
- **Data quality tests** on every model: not null, unique keys, accepted values, referential integrity, row counts within tolerance. Experiment-specific: a sample ratio mismatch test on the arm split, and a check that no visitor appears in both arms.
- **Version control.** `main` is protected. Work happens on branches and merges through pull requests. Each pull request carries review notes written against a checklist: tests added, dictionary updated, no hard-coded paths, seed fixed. One person fills both the author and reviewer roles. The notes are still written, because the discipline is the point.
- **Reproducibility.** Pinned dependencies, fixed seeds, a setup README, and a single command that regenerates the data, builds the models, runs the tests, and produces the reports. CI runs the same command on every pull request with a small seed.

### 7.3 Workstream 1: A/B test method

- **Unit of randomisation:** visitor. A visitor sees one checkout for the whole test.
- **Primary metric:** visitor-level checkout completion rate. **Secondary:** revenue per checkout visitor, average order value, Meridian Pay take-up. **Guardrails:** day-30 instalment miss rate on Meridian Pay orders, Meridian Pay decline rate, risk mix by internal credit band.
- **The analysis plan is written before results are seen.** Hypotheses, metrics, minimum detectable effect, sample size, duration, and stop rules go in the experiment design document, signed by the Product Owner and Finance (D-005). One fixed-horizon readout. No peeking.
- **Test window:** the synthetic test ran from Monday 1 June to Sunday 14 June 2026, so day-30 repayment outcomes are complete in the data. A minimum of two full weeks covers the weekly cycle whatever the power analysis says.
- **Stop rule (indicative; Finance confirms as D-004):** if the test arm's day-30 miss rate exceeds control by more than 0.5 percentage points and the 90% confidence interval for the difference excludes zero, the redesign does not ship as it stands. If the share of orders in the two highest-risk credit bands rises by more than 2 percentage points in the test arm, Finance reviews before any ship decision.
- **Test:** two-proportion z-test with confidence intervals for the primary metric, and the same for the guardrail. Sample ratio mismatch is checked before any result is read.

### 7.4 Workstream 2: Causal inference method

- **Estimand:** ATT of Circle membership on monthly spend over the six months after joining.
- **Primary method:** difference-in-differences with staggered adoption, estimated as an event study with an estimator that is robust to heterogeneous treatment timing (Callaway and Sant'Anna, or stacked regression), using not-yet-joined and never-joined customers as controls. A standard two-way fixed effects regression is reported alongside it only to show why it is not the primary estimate.
- **Robustness:** propensity score matching on pre-join spend, order frequency, tenure, category mix, and region; nearest-neighbour matching with a caliper; balance table before and after.
- **Placebo and sensitivity:** fake join dates six months before the real ones; alternative control groups; trimming of extreme spenders.
- **Readout:** effect in £ per member per month with a confidence interval, in the Data Analyst's words, then Finance's ROI.

## 10. Risks, assumptions, constraints

### Risks

| ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | The redesign raises Meridian Pay missed payments | Medium | High | Guardrail metric, stop rule, risk-mix check; Finance can hold a ship decision | Finance |
| R2 | Self-selection is mistaken for a loyalty effect | High | High | Event-study pre-trends, matching balance, placebo test, comparison with the planted ground truth | Data Engineer |
| R3 | Synthetic data is too clean, so methods look better than they would in practice | Medium | Medium | Inject duplicates, missing values, late repayments, seasonality; Data Analyst reviews realism; limits stated in the reports | Data Engineer, Data Analyst |
| R4 | Scope creep: extra segments, extra metrics, a dashboard | High | Medium | Business Analyst logs and traces every request; Product Owner decides; Definition of Ready gate | Business Analyst, Product Owner |
| R5 | One person playing five roles blurs voices and skips conflict | Medium | High | Voice guide in section 6; every conflict written out and logged; retrospective checks the log | Product Owner |
| R6 | Results read early or repeatedly, inflating false positives | Medium | Medium | Analysis plan signed before results; single fixed-horizon readout | Product Owner |
| R7 | Standard two-way fixed effects DiD is biased under staggered adoption | Medium | High | Estimator robust to heterogeneous timing as the primary method; TWFE shown only as a comparison | Data Engineer |
| R8 | The guardrail is underpowered: the day-30 miss rate is a rare outcome on the 24% of orders that use Meridian Pay, so a two-week test gives a wide interval on a 0.5 point difference | High | High | Power analysis for the guardrail, not just the primary metric, in Sprint 2; Finance chooses between a longer test, the risk-mix proxy, or accepting the interval (D-004) | Finance, Data Engineer |
| R9 | Eight weeks runs over alongside other commitments | Medium | Medium | Fortnightly reprioritisation; cut list agreed with the Product Owner; dates fixed, scope flexes | Product Owner, Data Engineer |

### Assumptions

- A1: The baselines in section 3 stand until Finance confirms or changes them (D-003, Sprint 1).
- A2: The synthetic checkout test ran 1 to 14 June 2026, so day-30 repayment outcomes are complete in the data.
- A3: Circle enrolment is staggered from February 2025. Customers who joined by February 2026 have a full six-month post-join window.
- A4: Randomisation was done correctly at visitor level. The sample ratio mismatch test verifies this.
- A5: No real customer data enters the repository at any point.

### Constraints

- C1: Direct tooling cost is £0 unless Finance signs off otherwise.
- C2: Everything in the repository is public. No real names, employers, or customers appear anywhere.
- C3: Since 15 July 2026, deferred payment credit provided by third-party lenders has been FCA-regulated. Merchant-provided credit such as Meridian Pay sits outside that perimeter. The team applies the FCA's disclosure and affordability standards as its own bar regardless: no checkout variant may reduce the visibility of repayment amounts, due dates, or the consequences of a missed payment. Finance owns this constraint. Source: FCA, "Regulating Buy Now Pay Later", fca.org.uk.
- C4: UK GDPR would apply to a real version of this work. The generator produces no personal data, and the pipeline is built as if it did: customer identifiers are surrogate keys and there are no free-text fields.
- C5: The eight-week window is fixed.

## 11. Governance

### 11.1 Definition of Ready

A backlog item can enter a sprint when:

- The problem it solves is written in the Business Analyst's words.
- Acceptance criteria are written and testable.
- It traces to a workstream and an objective in section 3.
- The data it needs is named.
- It is sized (S, M, L).
- Finance has no open question on cost or risk.

### 11.2 Definition of Done

A backlog item is done when:

- Code is on `main` through a pull request with review notes.
- Data quality tests pass in CI.
- The data dictionary covers any new field or metric, with lineage.
- Any number produced has been checked by the Data Analyst before it is reported.
- Any decision or result is logged.
- The next standup and the next stakeholder email reflect it.

### 11.3 Decision log

Each entry records: ID, date, the conflict, the options, the decision, the rationale, who decided, who was consulted, and any follow-up. Dissent is recorded, not smoothed over. A decision is reopened only with new evidence.

### 11.4 Escalation

1. Raise it in the Blockers line of the next standup.
2. If it is still open at the following standup, each side writes one paragraph: position and evidence.
3. The Product Owner decides within the sprint. Finance holds a veto only on guardrail thresholds, £ figures, and tooling cost. The Data Analyst can hold back a number that fails a quality check, and the Data Engineer fixes the cause in the same sprint.
4. The decision goes in the log with the dissent noted.

### 11.5 Change control

Changes to this charter go through a pull request to `/charter`, a version bump, and a decision log entry.
